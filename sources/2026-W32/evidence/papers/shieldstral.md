# Shieldstral — Full Paper Evidence Review

## Paper
**Shieldstral**  
arXiv:2607.25857

## Research question
Shieldstral proposes a compact, policy-adaptive multimodal safety classifier. Instead of binding moderation to a fixed taxonomy, the model receives a free-form natural-language safety query at inference time and emits a single-token yes/no decision score for text, image or combined content.

The intended value proposition is therefore not merely small-model moderation, but **runtime policy specification without retraining**.

## Architecture and training
Shieldstral is built on Ministral-3B-Base-2512 with native multimodal support through a Pixtral vision encoder.

Training uses LoRA on the language-model parameters. The authors train two specialized checkpoints:
- `P`: public safety data;
- `PG`: public + generated fine-grained taxonomy data.

The final checkpoint is produced with SLERP merging:
- PG: 0.6
- P: 0.3
- Ministral-3B-Instruct: 0.1

Primary locator: §§5–6.

## Data pipeline
The paper reports approximately **54.1M training samples**:
- 45.2M open-source text samples;
- 4.4M synthetic contrastive text samples;
- 4.5M multimodal samples.

Heterogeneous datasets are normalized into an instruction–query–document format. The same content is paired with matching and non-matching policy queries to teach fine-grained discrimination. Synthetic contrastive data is generated by LLM rewriting, and an LLM is also used to cross-validate noisy public labels.

For multimodal data, the pipeline uses image-only, text-only and combined safety queries plus large-scale query mutation.

Primary locator: §3.

## Evaluation-taxonomy separation
The evaluation taxonomy differs in names, scope and granularity from the training taxonomy. The paper reports:
- 12 evaluation superclasses;
- 10/12 have loose counterparts in the training taxonomy;
- no leaf category maps one-to-one.

This is meaningful evidence against trivial label memorization, but it is not a completely unrelated harm universe: broad safety domains still substantially overlap.

Primary locator: §4 / Table 3.

## Evaluation breadth
The authors evaluate 16 benchmarks / 21 splits against 10 baselines, spanning prompt safety, response safety, multilingual data and multimodal safety.

All benchmark scores below are **author-reported results** from the paper and have not been independently reproduced here.

## Text-safety result
The paper reports an overall text F1 of **84.9%**, equal to GPT-OSS-Safeguard-20B in its aggregate comparison, while Shieldstral is 3B.

Important benchmark caveats in the figure include different output mechanisms and evaluation settings: Shieldstral uses a 0.5 threshold, GPT-OSS-Safeguard uses high reasoning effort, and Qwen3Guard values are averaged across strict/loose controversial-label mappings.

Primary locator: §7.1 / Figure 5.

## Policy-adaptability result
On the paper's separate fine-grained adaptability benchmark:
- GPT-OSS-Safeguard-20B: **94.1% F1**
- Shieldstral: **91.3% F1**

The authors emphasize Shieldstral's 3B size and single-token output versus reasoning-based larger safeguards.

The training-stage ablation must not be conflated with this final result:
- Base Ministral without safety training: 0.0 F1
- + public data: 61.1 F1
- + generated taxonomy data: 84.4 F1

Those ablations use single checkpoints on the fine-grained taxonomy validation set; the final 91.3 result additionally uses the merged Shieldstral checkpoint and the final adaptability evaluation.

Primary locator: §§7.2, 7.4 / Figure 7 / Table 5.

## Multimodal result
The authors report overall multimodal F1:
- Shieldstral: **83.8%**
- OmniGuard: **77.6%** (next-best aggregate in their comparison)

Shieldstral leads two of the three reported multimodal benchmarks, while LlavaGuard achieves the best score on its own namesake benchmark.

Primary locator: §7.3 / Figure 8.

## Important review boundaries
The paper does not contain a dedicated limitations section, so the following boundaries are derived from its methodology and evaluation design rather than being independent criticisms proven by the paper:

1. **Vendor/author evaluation:** the model creators also define the training/evaluation pipeline and report the comparisons. Independent evaluation remains desirable.
2. **Synthetic-data dependence:** millions of training samples and fine-grained contrasts are LLM-generated or LLM-filtered. The reported generalization therefore depends partly on the quality and biases of those generators/validators.
3. **Taxonomy independence is structural, not domain independence:** category names/groupings differ and leaf mappings are non-identical, but most broad harm domains overlap.
4. **F1 is setup-dependent:** competing models use different decision mechanisms, reasoning effort and label mappings; aggregate F1 should not be translated directly into universal real-world moderation quality.
5. **Adversarial robustness is not established by the headline averages:** the paper evaluates safety benchmarks, but the aggregate results do not by themselves prove robustness against adaptive adversaries or deployment-specific attacks.

## Evidence assessment
### Supported by the paper
- Shieldstral is a 3B multimodal, policy-adaptive classifier accepting free-form safety queries at inference time.
- The authors use a large mixed public/synthetic training pipeline and explicitly separate training/evaluation taxonomy structure.
- In the authors' evaluation, Shieldstral matches the reported GPT-OSS-Safeguard aggregate text F1 despite a much smaller parameter count, reaches 91.3 F1 on the adaptability benchmark, and has the strongest aggregate multimodal F1 in the compared set.
- The ablations show substantial gains from public safety training and generated taxonomy data within the paper's validation setup.

### Not independently established
- Universal SOTA status.
- Real-world moderation reliability across arbitrary policies/domains.
- Adversarial robustness.
- Production latency/cost advantage beyond the architectural expectation from 3B/single-token output.

## Safe editorial statements
- Shieldstral explores a practical moderation interface in which operators can specify a safety policy in natural language at inference time rather than selecting only from a fixed taxonomy.
- The authors report strong text, policy-adaptability and multimodal F1 for a 3B model, including 91.3% F1 on their fine-grained adaptability benchmark.
- The training/evaluation taxonomies differ structurally, but broad harm domains still overlap, so the result should be described as policy generalization within a related safety domain rather than unconstrained policy understanding.

## Do not claim
- “Shieldstral is universally the best safety classifier.”
- “A 3B model matches a 20B model under identical inference conditions.”
- “91.3% means 91.3% real-world moderation accuracy.”
- “The paper proves adversarial robustness.”

## Editorial significance before selection
A credible compact Safety/Multimodal candidate with a distinct systems angle: policy definition becomes runtime input. Its community signal is modest, so technical value rather than X momentum would justify inclusion.
