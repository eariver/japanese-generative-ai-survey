## 1. What was announced (article-level, publisher-attributed)

AstaBrief 8B: a small open model that turns a research question + retrieved literature
excerpts into a cited report, deployed as Asta "Generate a report" Fast mode alongside
Claude-powered Thinking mode, with weights + training data + example local-PDF workflow
released for study/reproduction. Publisher-framed goal: match proprietary report quality
at far lower time/cost; open weights let institutions run behind their own firewall.
Article: "We built AstaBrief 8B, a model that turns a research question and retrieved
literature excerpts into a cited report" and "we're also open-sourcing it and the
training data so others can study, reproduce, and build on our approach."

## 2. Base + post-training (article-declared method)

- Base: Qwen3-8B; post-training ONLY (no new pretraining claim): SFT then DPO.
  RL-based training was explicitly considered and set aside as "unstable and expensive";
  the team tested "how far we could push report generation quality with a cheaper, more
  operationally manageable setup."
- Query pool: 90K research-focused queries after filtering (beta/bot/short removed +
  GPT-4o pass dropping non-English, non-scientific, personal-medical-info prompts;
  per SFT dataset card, filter uses a published
  [query_filter_prompt](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/AstaBrief_prompts/blob/main/query_filter_prompt.txt)).
  Source queries: real opted-in user queries via OpenScholar / Asta ScholarQA systems
  (to June 2025).
- Targets: full cited reports generated by the multi-step ScholarQA pipeline
  (retrieve → organize → synthesize) backed by Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, o3, o4-mini, GPT-4.1;
  quality-filtered.
- Pipeline redesign: full report written in ONE pass rather than section-by-section,
  bypassing snippet summarization/clustering stages (article attributes much of the
  speedup to this + small-model serving), "without sacrificing performance."

## 3. Data lineage CORRECTED (F05): 47K usable vs public 39.5K mix (dataset-card verified)

Previous (r12) wording left the 47K→39.5K relation ambiguous. The public
[SFT Mix dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/AstaBrief_SFT_Mix)
(retrieved 2026-10-10) states the lineage exactly; the correction below follows the
card, not inference:

- 90K filtered query pool → report generation + quality filtering (e.g. dropping
  reports with very few references) → **47K usable training examples, AFTER setting
  some aside for DPO data generation** (card: "this yielded 47K usable training
  examples after setting some aside for DPO data generation").
- Citation-density filter: density = share of statements with ≥1 citation; reports
  with **density < 0.25 were filtered out, leaving 39.5k examples** (card, verbatim
  threshold — the 0.25 figure is used here ONLY because the dataset card states it;
  the article prose itself names the filter but gives no number).
- Therefore: the public 39.5K SFT Mix IS the density≥0.25 subset of the 47K usable
  set (minus DPO-held-aside queries), per the card — not an independently invented
  causal chain. Do not rephrase as "47K collected, 39.5K released" without the
  density-filter + DPO-carve-out intermediates.
- Live card preview rows corroborate the filter's effect (sampled
  `citation_density_sentences`: 0.41, 0.37, 0.37, 0.35, 0.39, 0.49 — all ≥ 0.25).
  Card viewer showed a transient `JobManagerCrashedError` on full preview; card prose
  (not the preview) is the authority cited here.

## 4. DPO pairs CORRECTED (F05): ~6K vs 6,622 rows, judges, 95% hedge

- Article: "the final DPO dataset came to about 6K examples", built from a held-out
  query subset. One side: ScholarQA pipeline report (typically Claude 3.5/3.7-backed);
  other side: same retrieved excerpts fed to o3, o4-mini, DeepSeek-V3, or DeepSeek-R1.
- Public [DPO Mix card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/AstaBrief_DPO_Mix)
  (retrieved 2026-10-10): **6,622 rows** (viewer + "Number of rows: 6,622"), 12K query
  pool "after setting 47k aside for SFT", dual-judge agreement filtering → 6.6k.
  Viewer `chosen_model`/`rejected_model` tags (e.g. `sqa+claude37` vs
  `llm-only_DeepSeek-V3-0324`) corroborate the pipeline-vs-single-model pairing shape.
- Judges: GPT-4.1 and DeepSeek-R1 with a general report-comparison prompt (card links
  the [pairwise_judge_prompt](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/AstaBrief_prompts/blob/main/pairwise_judge_prompt.txt));
  only pairs where BOTH judges agreed were kept.
- 95% figure, HEDGED per r13 instruction: article and card both state judges "aligned
  with human preferences (95% agreement)", but **neither the article prose nor the
  dataset card discloses the alignment study's sample size or exact conditions**.
  Cite as "claimed 95% judge–human agreement (sample size/conditions undisclosed in
  checked primary sources)", never as a measured-with-N fact.
- Design rationale (article): "multiple generators and requiring agreement between two
  judges … without treating any single model's output or judgment as ground truth."

## 5. Reported numbers WITH conditions (never cross-harness ranking)

- End-to-end Asta pipeline: Fast mode 51.1s/report vs Thinking mode 178.5s (≈3.5×).
  The article ALSO frames "nearly an order-of-magnitude reduction in report generation
  time compared to the proprietary models we tracked" — that framing is about the
  **generation-time component**, distinct from the 3.5× end-to-end figure. Keep the two
  framings separate exactly as the article does.
- Development evals: SQABench-CS2 (200 user-written CS research questions; four
  tracked metrics — rubric score/coverage, answer precision, citation precision,
  citation recall) + DeepScholarBench (63-query long-form synthesis; "its own metrics,
  which are not comparable with SQABench-CS2's" — article caption, never compare).
  LLM-judged pairwise comparison vs Thinking-mode reference + 14-question human study
  (3 researchers, 4–5 questions each, ties allowed): overall preference DR-Tulu wins;
  2/3 researchers preferred AstaBrief on citation accuracy.
- Article's own limits (must be preserved): "Most of the training and evaluation
  described was completed in 2025" — proprietary data-generators and comparison points
  reflect that frontier; "We haven't rerun the full evaluation against today's frontier
  models"; read as evidence about training/system choices, not SOTA proof.
- Early-usage figures (bounded extra, article-reported, sparse-feedback caveat):
  374 Fast-mode triallists, 29.1% used 2+ days, 3.67 threads mean, 23% never switched
  back, 18% mix Fast/Thinking, positive-feedback 84.2% vs 85.2% ("feedback is generally
  too sparse to draw strong conclusions" — article's own caveat, preserved).
- Grounding lesson the article stresses: citation support ≠ scope preservation
  (sample→population, past→present-tense, descriptive→recommendation drift; article
  links the generalization-bias mechanism). The dev metrics covered relevance/coverage/
  citation grounding; scope-preservation testing is future work ("evaluations that go
  beyond whether a claim has a supporting citation to ask whether a model preserves the
  evidentiary scope of its sources"). SFT improved quality but lagged Claude on
  precision/citation until density filtering + DPO closed the gap.
- Keep technically distinct: grounding (claims supported), citation density (share of
  statements cited — a DATA filter), citation precision (citations support attached
  claims), answer precision (paragraph relevance), scope preservation (no broadening).
  A densely cited report can still overstate — density rewards citing, not scope fidelity.
