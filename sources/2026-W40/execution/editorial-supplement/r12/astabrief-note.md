## 1. What was announced (article-level, publisher-attributed)

AstaBrief 8B: a small open model that turns a research question + retrieved literature
excerpts into a cited report, deployed as Asta "Generate a report" Fast mode alongside
Claude-powered Thinking mode, with weights + training data + example local-PDF workflow
released for study/reproduction. Publisher-framed goal: match proprietary report quality
at far lower time/cost; open weights let institutions run behind their own firewall.

## 2. Base + post-training (article-declared method)

- Base: Qwen3-8B; post-training ONLY (no new pretraining claim): SFT then DPO.
- SFT data: 47K usable full-report examples. Source queries: real opted-in user queries
  via OpenScholar / Asta ScholarQA systems (to June 2025), filtered (beta/bot/short
  removed). Targets: full cited reports generated by the multi-step ScholarQA pipeline
  (retrieve → organize → synthesize) backed by Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, o3, o4-mini, GPT-4.1;
  quality-filtered. Key filter lesson (article's own): citation-density filtering gave the
  clear gains; fancier combinations + LR sweeps added little.
- DPO data: ~6K pairs from held-out queries. One side: ScholarQA pipeline report
  (typically Claude 3.5/3.7-backed); other side: same retrieved excerpts fed to a
  different model (o3, o4-mini, DeepSeek-V3/R1). Preference label: agreement of two
  judge models (article states multi-generator + dual-judge design avoids single-model
  ground truth).
- Pipeline redesign: full report written in ONE pass rather than section-by-section
  (article attributes much of the speedup to this + small-model serving).

## 3. Reported numbers WITH conditions (never cross-harness ranking)

- End-to-end Asta pipeline: Fast mode 51.1s/report vs Thinking mode 178.5s (≈3.5×;
  article also frames "nearly an order of magnitude" for generation-time component —
  keep the two framings distinct as the article does).
- Development evals (LLM-judged + 14-question human study, 3 researchers):
  competitive with Claude pipeline and DR Tulu on answer/citation quality; 2/3
  researchers preferred AstaBrief on citation accuracy; DR-Tulu wins overall preference.
  Benchmarks named: SQABench-CS2 (CS research questions) and DeepScholarBench
  (long-form synthesis; metrics NOT comparable across the two).
- Article's own limits (must be preserved): most training/eval completed in 2025 —
  proprietary data-generators and comparison points reflect that frontier; NOT rerun
  against today's frontier; read as evidence about training/system choices, not SOTA proof.
- Grounding lesson the article stresses: citation support ≠ scope preservation
  (sample→population, past→present-tense, descriptive→recommendation drift). The dev
  metrics covered relevance/coverage/citation grounding; scope-preservation testing is
  future work. SFT improved quality but lagged Claude on precision/citation until
  density filtering + DPO closed the gap.
