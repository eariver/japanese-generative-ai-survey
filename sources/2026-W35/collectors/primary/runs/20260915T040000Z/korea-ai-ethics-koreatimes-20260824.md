Title: [COVER STORY] Korea's AI ethics principles lack binding force, specificity. By Lee Gyu-lee, Aug 24 2026 (upd. Aug 25).

Government announced national AI ethics principles Monday (finalized Aug 21): 3 core values — human dignity (people as ends, not means), common good (social trust/collective interest), sustainability of humanity (present + future generations). 7 guidelines: human-centeredness, privacy protection, fairness and inclusiveness, accountability, safety, reliability, transparency. Nonbinding common reference for developers/providers/users/agencies/civil society (MSIT).

Minister Bae Kyung-hoon: "healthy AI ecosystem where technological innovation and responsibility go hand in hand"; success = AI put to use across industries and everyday life.

Principle content: human-centeredness (support, not replace, human judgment; define AI role; allow intervention; guard overreliance); privacy (specified purposes, necessary scope); fairness/inclusiveness; accountability (clear assignment among actors); safety/reliability (risk management + performance standards per purpose); transparency (use/limitations/decision criteria, esp. when rights/obligations/opportunities affected).

Expert reception: Choi Kyoung-jin (Gachon University AI/data/policy center): broad declaration of values, no sanctions; effectiveness depends on sector-specific translation. Industry official: mostly restates basics companies do anyway; limited immediate practical impact.

Next: government to develop case-based guidance + sector-specific self-guided checklists.
